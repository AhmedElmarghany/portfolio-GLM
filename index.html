01 — About

Engineering rigor,
designer's judgment.

I studied Computers & Systems Engineering at the Faculty of Engineering, Mansoura University, graduating in July 2025. Control systems teach you to think in feedback loops — inputs, outputs, and what happens in between. It turns out that's a useful way to think about software products too.

Along the way I gravitated toward the part of engineering people actually see: the interface. I learned to build with React, Next.js, and TypeScript, then kept going deeper on both sides — Node.js and Django on the backend — because understanding the whole stack makes you better at designing the part users touch.

I also work professionally on a real enterprise web application built with Django: redesigning existing interfaces, implementing features, and improving usability inside a codebase I didn't create. It's taught me as much about software as any greenfield project ever could — how to work within constraints, respect existing patterns, and still raise the bar.

Usability, aesthetics, and maintainability aren't competing goals — they're the same goal, seen from different sides.

I've worked in teams and led one — coordinating a graduation project from planning through delivery — and I care about the unglamorous parts: clear communication, code other people can read, and designs that hold up under real content.
